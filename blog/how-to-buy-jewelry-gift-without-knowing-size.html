Quick answer

If you don't know the person's size, the safest jewelry gift is one that doesn't need a size at all: stud or hoop earrings, an ear cuff, a pendant necklace, or an open bangle. If it has to be a ring, find the size quietly first by borrowing a ring they already wear on the right finger, asking someone close to them, or using a retailer's sizing guide against a ring you can measure. When you still have to guess, pick a plain, simple band that a jeweler can resize, buy from a retailer with a clear return or resizing policy, and ask for a gift receipt.

Key takeaways

  • The easiest way to avoid a sizing problem is to choose a type of jewelry where size barely matters, such as earrings, ear cuffs or a pendant.
  • For rings, a ring the person already wears on the same finger is the most reliable clue you can get without asking them directly.
  • Plain metal bands are usually the easiest rings to resize; full eternity bands and some intricate designs may not be resizable at all.

Start with pieces that don't need a size

Most sizing stress comes from deciding on a ring first and then trying to solve the size problem. Flip the order. Ask what kind of jewelry would make a good gift for this person, and see whether a size-free option fits just as well.

Earrings

Earrings are the classic no-size gift. A pair of small studs or simple hoops works with most wardrobes and most ages, and there is nothing to measure. The two things you do need to know are whether the person has pierced ears, and whether they tend to wear gold, silver or rose tones. Our diamond earrings guide and gold hoop earrings guide go deeper into styles and sizes.

Ear cuffs

Ear cuffs sit on the outer rim of the ear and are generally designed to be worn without an extra piercing, which makes them a forgiving gift for someone who likes a stacked look but may not want new holes. If the person has sensitive skin, read our guide to the best sterling silver ear cuffs for sensitive ears, and if they already wear several earrings, our ear cuffs for multiple piercings guide shows how cuffs sit alongside existing studs and hoops.

Pendant necklaces

A pendant necklace only asks you to choose a length, and as the length guide further down shows, most adults can wear a mid-length chain comfortably.

Open bangles and cuff bracelets

An open bangle or cuff has a gap at the back, so it slips on from the side of the wrist rather than over the hand. That gap gives a little flexibility that a closed bangle doesn't have. Check the inner measurements on the listing where they are given.

How to find their ring size without asking

If a ring really is the right gift, it is worth spending a little effort on the size before you buy. These methods run from most to least reliable.

1. Borrow a ring they already wear on the right finger

The best evidence is a ring the person wears regularly on the same finger you are buying for. A jeweler can measure it in a minute, or you can check it against the retailer's sizing guide. Make sure it is the same finger on the same hand, since left and right hands often differ slightly.

2. Measure the inside of a ring at home

If you can't take the ring out of the house, measure its inside diameter edge to edge with a ruler marked in millimeters, measuring across the widest part of the inside of the band, not the outside. You can then compare the number with the chart below or with the retailer's own size chart. You can also press it gently into a bar of soap and measure the impression later.

3. Ask someone close to them

A close friend, sibling or parent may already know the size, or can find out naturally while shopping together.

4. Just ask, with a cover story or without one

There is nothing wrong with asking. If the surprise matters less than the fit, a direct question avoids a return.

If you still have to guess

If there is no ring to borrow and no one to ask, reduce the cost of being wrong instead of trying to be exactly right.

Choose a design that can be resized

Plain metal bands and simple solitaire settings are usually the easiest to resize. Rings with stones set all the way around the band, such as full eternity bands, are often difficult or impossible to resize because there is no plain section of metal to cut and rejoin. Avoid them when you are guessing.

Think about the season

Fingers tend to be slightly smaller in cold weather and slightly larger in heat, after exercise or late in the day. When you are shopping in November and December, keep that in mind and don't size down just because a ring looked loose on a cold day.

For a proposal, separate the ring from the moment

If you are planning to propose and can't confirm the size, you can still propose with the ring and have it resized afterwards. Bracelets when you don't know their wrist size

Bracelets are more forgiving than rings, but fit still affects how often a bracelet gets worn.

  • Chain bracelets: look for an extender chain or several closing points. A common rule of thumb is to add about half an inch to an inch to the wrist measurement for a comfortable chain bracelet, depending on how loosely the person likes to wear it.
  • Tennis bracelets: these sit close to the wrist and have little give. If you are considering one, our tennis bracelet guide covers fit and clasp types in more detail.
  • Open bangles and cuffs: the opening allows a little flexibility. Check the inner dimensions on the listing and compare them with a bracelet they already own.
  • Closed bangles: these must pass over the hand, so measure around the widest part of the hand with the fingers held together, or measure a bangle they already wear. Avoid closed bangles if you can't do either.

If you can borrow a bracelet they wear often, lay it flat and measure it end to end, including the clasp.

Earrings and ear cuffs: what to check instead of size

Earrings remove the sizing question, but a few other details decide whether they get worn.

  • Piercings: studs, hoops and drops need pierced ears. Ear cuffs and clip-on styles don't.
  • Metal sensitivity: some people react to certain metals, nickel being a common example. If they mention irritation from cheaper jewelry, choose pieces where the listing clearly states the metal, such as sterling silver or solid gold, and check the description of any plating.
  • Hoop size: small hoops that hug the lobe are easy to wear every day; large hoops are more of a statement. When in doubt, go smaller.
  • Backs and closures: secure backs matter for valuable earrings. Spare backs are inexpensive and make a thoughtful extra.

Read their jewelry box before you buy

The best clue to what someone will wear is what they already wear. Notice it for a few days, or look through recent photos, with this checklist.

  1. Metal color: do they wear mostly yellow-toned metal, white metals such as silver and white gold, rose tones, or a mix? Matching their usual metal is one of the simplest ways to make a gift feel right.
  2. Scale: are their pieces delicate and fine, or bold and chunky? Stay close to the scale they already choose.
  3. Work and lifestyle: people who work with their hands, care for small children or exercise often may prefer low-profile pieces without prongs that catch on fabric.
  4. Sentiment: do they wear initials, birthstones or charms? A personal detail can matter more than the size of a stone.

Returns, resizing and holiday timing

When you buy jewelry for someone else without knowing their size, the retailer's policies matter as much as the piece itself. Before you pay, work through these checks.

  1. Return window: find out how many days you have to return or exchange the piece, and whether holiday purchases get a longer window. A gift given on December 25 may not be tried on until days later.
  2. Gift receipt: ask whether the retailer offers a gift receipt or a gift note without prices, so the recipient can exchange the piece without you.
  3. Resizing policy: for rings, check whether resizing is offered, whether the first resize is free, how long it takes and whether it affects the warranty.
  4. Final sale items: engraved, personalized and custom pieces are often non-returnable. Avoid engraving a ring you are not sure will fit.
  5. Delivery date: check the retailer's holiday shipping cut-off and estimated delivery, especially for international retailers and made-to-order fine jewelry, which can take longer.

Common buying mistakes

  • Measuring the wrong finger or hand: a ring from the middle finger or the other hand can be a different size from the ring finger you are buying for.
  • Measuring the outside of a ring: always measure the inside diameter, not the outside edge of the band.
  • Engraving before you know it fits: personalized pieces are often final sale, and engraving can complicate resizing.
  • Choosing a non-resizable design when guessing: full eternity bands and some intricate settings can't easily be adjusted.
  • Forgetting about pierced ears: studs and hoops are no use to someone without piercings; an ear cuff may be the better choice.
  • Leaving it too late: fine jewelry can take longer to make and ship than you expect, and returns or resizing take time too.

Bottom line

You don't need to know someone's size to give them jewelry they will love. Start by asking whether earrings, an ear cuff, a pendant or an open bangle would suit them just as well as a ring, because those gifts remove the sizing risk entirely. If it has to be a ring, borrow one they wear on the same finger, ask someone close to them, or measure carefully against the retailer's chart, and choose a simple design that can be resized. Whatever you choose, check the return and resizing policy, ask for a gift receipt, and order early.

Frequently asked questions

What is the safest jewelry gift if I don't know their size?

Earrings, ear cuffs and pendant necklaces are the safest choices because they don't depend on a ring or wrist size. Studs and hoops only require pierced ears, and ear cuffs are generally worn without an extra piercing. An open bangle is a good bracelet option because the gap gives some flexibility.

How can I find out someone's ring size without them knowing?

Borrow a ring they wear on the same finger and have a jeweler measure it, or measure its inside diameter in millimeters and compare it with the retailer's size chart. You can also ask a close friend or family member.

Should I size up or down if I have to guess a ring size?

If you have to guess, choose a simple band that can be resized and buy from a retailer with a clear resizing or exchange policy. Wide bands tend to fit more snugly than thin ones, so many jewelers suggest going up slightly for a wide band.

Can a ring be resized after I give it as a gift?

Many rings can be resized, especially plain metal bands and simple solitaire settings. Rings with stones all the way around the band, tension settings and some intricate designs may be difficult or impossible to resize. Check the retailer's resizing policy, cost and timing before you buy.

What should I check before buying jewelry as a holiday gift?

Check the return or exchange window, whether a gift receipt is available, the resizing policy for rings, whether engraved or personalized items are final sale, and the retailer's holiday shipping cut-off and estimated delivery date.
